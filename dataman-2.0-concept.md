# Dataman 2.0 — Project Concept Notes

## Class Context
- Course: Systems Analysis and Design
- Assignment: Program a modern version of the classic Dataman educational math toy
- Phases: Individual build first, then team-based
- Framework being used: **Understand → Identify → Gather → Define → Recommend**

## Origin of the Idea
- Professor shared that Dataman was personally meaningful to him as a kid — a gift from his father, who passed away 2 years ago
- Dataman worked because it was physical and tangible: kids could touch it, interact with it, and being wrong wasn't crushing — it was "just something that happened"
- Core class question raised: as the world moves digital, how do you preserve that same sense of imagination and safe interaction that a physical object provided?
- Professor proposed a Tamagotchi-style approach for the modern version

## Core Concept
**The relationship is inverted.** In the original, Dataman helps the kid learn math. In Dataman 2.0, the player has grown up, and now Dataman needs *them* — found broken/dusty in an old box, no longer functioning as a physical device (representing a changing world), and the player must bring him back by transferring his "soul" into a digital machine.

This preserves the original purpose (building real math skill through hands-on interaction) while evolving *what the relationship means* — not a tool being used, but a partnership.

## Purpose (working draft)
- **Core/unchanged purpose:** help build real math fluency through hands-on, low-stakes interaction (same as original Dataman)
- **Expanded purpose (ambitious/long-term vision):** brains and machine working together — not just learning math, but learning *how to learn/study*, with Dataman as a partner in that process
- Note: the choice between "math fluency as the star" vs. "learning-how-to-learn as the star" is currently being weighed against build feasibility/timeline, not decided on merit alone — worth keeping the ideal vision and the buildable scope as separate, explicit layers rather than collapsing them into one decision

## Narrative Frame
- **Antagonist: Anti-Math** — represents the idea that machines should do people's thinking for them (directly mirrors the real design risk of leaning too hard on AI/automation in the build itself)
- **Opening sequence:** player finds Dataman old and non-functional in a box; a Jumanji-style invitation (a short riddle/question, ideally math-flavored) appears and "revives" him
- Revival sequence doubles as the first gameplay moment — no separate intro mechanic needed, the riddle *is* the first math interaction
- Uses the classic Dataman boot-up sequence (blinking lights, familiar sounds) to trigger nostalgia, then deliberately complicates it — this is not the exact same object, but it still matters and still functions
- Emotional throughline: nostalgia as an entry point, immediately complicated rather than just indulged — mirrors growing up and re-examining childhood things with more nuance instead of either idealizing or dismissing them

## Design Philosophy / Constraints
- **Preserve friction** — inspired by Elden Ring: no hand-holding, no smoothing over struggle; learning happens through trying, failing, adjusting, trying again without being punished out of the experience
- **Bridge physical-to-digital** — original Dataman's power came from tactile interaction; digital version needs some equivalent of "touch it and it's okay to be wrong" rather than just typing an answer into a box
- **AI's role must stay narrow and supportive** — e.g. generating new problem variants — and must never become what Anti-Math represents (the machine doing the thinking instead of the kid)

## Tamagotchi/Companion Mechanic
- Ongoing relationship, not session-based — Dataman persists and needs continued care/attention over time
- Math practice ties into that care mechanic (exact implementation still open)
- Open question: does neglect have consequences? What does "showing up" look like mechanically?

## Requirements Framework Progress
- **Understand:** Core interaction loop of the original (press button → get problem → answer → get feedback) is the foundation; purpose stays the same at its root, but the relationship/framing evolves
- **Identify:** Not yet started
- **Gather:** Not yet started
- **Define:** Not yet started
- **Recommend:** Not yet started

## Scope Notes
- Solo build first — should be realistic to actually build/demo, not just designed on paper
- Full adaptive-AI, deep Tamagotchi mechanics, and expanded "learning how to learn" purpose may be more realistic for the team phase
- Suggested approach: define the ideal/long-term vision now, then let scope determine which honest slice of it gets built for the individual phase (not treated as a lesser version, just an earlier one)

## Open Questions
- What does Anti-Math actually *do* in-world? (Corrupt problems? Spread apathy? Convince others thinking isn't needed?)
- What is the actual riddle/question in the revival sequence?
- What replaces "solve a problem, get right/wrong feedback" as the core loop, if anything?
- Does neglecting Dataman have in-game consequences?
- How much of the "learning how to learn" vision is feasible for the individual-phase build vs. team phase?
